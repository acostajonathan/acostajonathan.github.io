Unimanual Robot – End of Arm Tool (EOAT) Design

1 / 3

Objective: Engineered a custom End-of-Arm Tool (EOAT) for a unimanual Autonomous Mobile Robot (AMR) manipulator to execute precise pick-and-place payload transfers of spool components.

Tools Used: Siemens NX

Challenges

  • Navigated strict spatial constraints to maintain less than 1-inch operational clearance between the spool and container walls.
  • Designed the component to resist mechanical deflection and prevent yielding during dynamic payload transfers.
  • Executed the complete design-to-manufacture lifecycle within an accelerated two-week deadline.

Results / Learnings

  • Modeled the EOAT geometry in Siemens NX, integrating custom ridged contact surfaces to maximize grip friction and mitigate slippage during operation.
  • Validated the design's structural integrity by conducting fundamental yield analysis under static loading conditions prior to fabrication.
  • Leveraged additive manufacturing to fabricate the final part in Nylon 11, satisfying both the high-strength material requirements and the strict timeline.

Bucket Weight Detection System – Safety & Ergonomics

1 / 3

Objective: Produced a spring-loaded scale mechanism to continuously monitor purge bucket capacity, alerting associates via an HMI to prevent overfill and enhance operational safety.

Tools Used: SolidWorks

Challenges

  • Alerted the HMI (Human Machine Interface) when the bucket reached a weight of 25 lbs. (Maximum allowable weight for operator safety)
  • Restricted the mechanism to a maximum height of 3 inches to prevent physical interference with the automated robot's operational path.
  • Designed in a way that prevented accidental triggering during maintenance.

Results / Learnings

  • Utilized SolidWorks to model the complete assembly, calculating the combined mass of the bucket and upper plate to specify a spring stiffness that achieves exactly 6mm of linear descent at the 25 lb. threshold.
  • Designated an inductive sensor to trigger the HMI when the bucket descended in a certain orientation to only detect that upper plate.

Cell Pickup Fixture – Quality & Efficiency

1 / 3

Objective: Applied Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T) to design a high-precision locating plate for automated cell pickup, preventing component damage and eliminating missed cells during gantry operations.

Tools Used: SolidWorks, Excel

Challenges

  • Investigated and identified the root cause of systemic gantry pickup errors that resulted in 3-4 missed cells per pallet on the second box.
  • Engineered a mechanical solution to secure cardboard cell boxes in a fixed, repeatable position relative to the automated gantry system.
  • Adapted existing assembly line constraints to integrate the new fixture without disrupting overarching manufacturing workflows.

Results / Learnings

  • Diagnosed positional drift caused by a missing physical datum between consecutive cardboard cell boxes.
  • Utilized SolidWorks and strict GD&T principles to design a robust locating plate featuring a 5mm retaining wall to ensure precise, repeatable positioning.
  • Integrated the physical solution by widening the slot of the overhead locator plate while reprogramming the automated pallet stopping coordinates to accommodate the new fixture.
