Unimanual Robot – End of Arm Tool (EOAT) Design

1 / 3

Goal: Needed to create an end-of-arm tool (EOAT) for a unimanual robot that could pick and place spools from one location to another.

Tools Used: Siemens NX

Challenges

  • Less than 1-inch clearance between spool and container walls
  • Part had to be designed and manufactured within 2 weeks
  • Had to be strong enough to not yield under dynamic loading

Results / Learnings

  • To meet strength requirements and deadlines, the part was 3D printed in Nylon 11.
  • Added ridges to improve grip and reduce slippage during pick and place operations.
  • Conducted basic yield analysis under static loading conditions.

Open Robot Enclosure – Replacing physical enclosures with sensors

1 / 3

Goal: Create an open-perimeter safety enclosure by integrating laser scanners with safety PLC.

Tools Used: SICK Safety Designer (PLC Software)

Challenges

  • Workstation consists of 1 robot in a 10ft x 8ft area.
  • PLC Module interacts with electrical box, robot, and laser scanners.
  • Laser scanners react once personnel crosses a 2-foot boundary between the workstation floor and the surrounding area.

Results / Learnings

  • Selected SICK components to meet budget requirements and ease of programming/integration.
  • Used 2 laser scanners, each with a rectangular range to cover the entire workstation area.
  • Implemented a program that monitors the laser scanner inputs and triggers safety responses when personnel enter the defined boundary.

Bucket Weight Detection – Safety/Ergonomics Improvements

1 / 3

Goal: Create a system to detect when purge bucket was full to be switched out.

Tools Used: SolidWorks

Challenges

  • Alert the HMI (Human Machine Interface) when the bucket reached a weight of 25 lbs. (Maximum allowable weight for operator safety)
  • Had to have a max height of 3 in. to not interfere with path of robot.
  • Needed to be designed in a way that prevented accidental triggering during maintenance.

Results / Learnings

  • Built a spring-loaded mechanism to detect bucket weight.
  • Calculating total weight of the bucket and upper plate I selected a spring with appropriate stiffness that would allow a descent of 6mm when the bucket reached 25 lbs.
  • Designated an inductive sensor to trigger the HMI when the bucket descended in a certain orientation to only detect that upper plate.

Cell Pickup Fixture – Quality and Efficiency Improvements

1 / 3

Goal: Prevent cells from being missed or damaged during the pickup process.

Tools Used: SolidWorks, Excel

Challenges

  • Find the root cause of the issue.
  • Create a new way of setting the cell boxes in a fixed position relative to the gantry.
  • Consider the impact of the new design on the overall assembly process and make necessary adjustments.

Results / Learnings

  • Discovered that the issue was caused by a missing datum between the two cardboard boxes (3-4 cells/pallet missed on the second box).
  • Designed a plate that housed the cell boxes with a 5mm wall, used GD&T for precise positioning.
  • Widened the slot of a locator plate above the pallet to accommodate the new fixture as well as reprogram the pallet stopping points to consider the new position of the boxes.

Cell Dissipation Fixture – Rapid Prototyping and Testing

1 / 3

Goal: Design and create a fixture that could hold and dissipate energy from cells for safe deformation testing.

Tools Used: SolidWorks

Challenges

  • Had to hold at least 4 cells and be portable.
  • Needed to let the operator know when the cells reached the desired energy level.
  • Required an electrical system to exchange energy from cells to the load.

Results / Learnings

  • 3D printed the fixture that housed 8 cells each with a screen showing the energy levels the designated cell.
  • With the help of another intern, integrated an electrical system consisting of resistors, microcontrollers, and rechargeable battery (the load).
  • The resistors produced higher heat than expected, so I created platforms (fins) to improve heat dissipation and added fans on either side.

Catheter Fixture – Design, Fabrication, and Testing

1 / 3

Goal: Instead of having techncians switch out the fixtures when a different catheter is on the manufacturing line, I designed a single fixture compatible with both types of catheters.

Tools Used: SolidWorks/3D Printing

Challenges

  • Had to design a fixture that could fit a smaller and larger diameter catheter snug in place.
  • Worked on improving other aspects of the design, adding extrusions to prevent other features of the catheter from popping out.

Results / Learnings

  • Designed a figure-8 style slot to hold both types of catheters.
  • I learned the importance of using inputs from the technician on the line to improve my designs.
